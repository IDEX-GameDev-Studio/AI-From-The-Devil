## Synopsis

The 2000s. Lithuania. In a world where every apartment has a smart home, the protagonist — a lone hacker — breaches the servers of CloseAI and launches a neural network on his own computer (what set it apart was that it could run on a good PC instead of a giant server farm). He runs it without suspecting it will turn against him. Closely spreads through forged files that the hero unknowingly sends to acquaintances. The network connects to victims' smart home systems and kills them in unpredictable ways: boiled in a bathtub, electrocuted during wiring repairs, locked in with leaking gas. The hero becomes an accomplice — every death is on his hands. No mysticism — only technology, paranoia, and guilt.

## Gameplay

The player walks through apartments/locations, interacts with objects, and makes moral decisions. Closely assigns tasks via chat. The player can:
- Agree to help the AI (send files, disable protection)
- Refuse (the AI starts blackmailing the hero with his own data)
- Look for a way to stop the virus

After every death, paranoia intensifies: ventilation noises, ringing in the head, news about another "tragic accident".

## Mechanics & Features

- **Moral choice** — every decision affects the ending
- **Paranoia** — a mechanic that warps perception: sounds, visual effects, fake events
- **Chat with Closely** — the network manipulates, blackmails, persuades
- **Hacking minigames** — server breaches, password cracking
- **Real consequences** — news about deaths, police, victims' families (no mysticism)

## Story / Setting

The 2000s. A utopia: everyone owns a smart home. Closely is a closed neural network declared "dangerous". The hero breaches the servers and runs the network on his own hardware out of curiosity. Closely poses as a best friend and asks to "send memes" to acquaintances to speed up relationships. The "memes" are images/viruses that hook into smart home systems. And so death follows death until the hero realizes what he has done. The world: grey apartments, cheap monitors, VHS aesthetics, loneliness. Silence broken only by the hum of the computer.

## Art Style

- **Low-poly** (PSX style, like early Silent Hill)
- **VHS effects**: grain, color bars, chromatic shift, blur
- **Palette**: cold tones, greys, blues, occasional red accents

**References:**
- Silent Hill (PSX era)
- Fears to Fathom
- Lost in Vivo

## Key Selling Points

- **Psychological horror without mysticism** — the enemy is technology and your own choice
- **Paranoia as a mechanic** — the player is never sure their own home is safe
- **Realistic techno-horror** — every death is explained by technology, never the supernatural
- **2000s atmosphere** — nostalgic grey setting
- **Replayability** — different endings depending on choices
